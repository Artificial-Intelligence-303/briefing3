## Where the reading comes from: IAAI

**IAAI**, Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence, is the conference AAAI runs for AI systems
that are in real use. Its papers are written by the people who built and deployed each system, and say
what happened once real people used it. Every paper in `papers.md` was presented at IAAI-26 and is free
to read. Four of them also appeared in AAAI's *AI Magazine* in August 2026.

## What this briefing practices

Three of the course learning outcomes:

- **Outcome 1:** "Correctly implement an intelligent agent and accurately describe its properties." You
  describe the system in your paper the way this course describes an agent: who runs it, what it
  perceives, and what it does.
- **Outcome 3:** "Design, implement, and assess an intelligent system for various artificial
  intelligence applications." This briefing practices the third part: you judge how the authors
  assessed their own system.
- **Outcome 5:** "Evaluate intelligent systems while considering their social, political, and ethical
  implications and communicate their outcomes in both written and oral forms." You say who pays when
  the system is wrong and what the paper leaves out, then brief the class.

## The three things that make it a briefing rather than a summary

**1. The claims, and how they were measured.** For each claim, give the number, what it is compared
against, and who measured it on how many cases or people. The builders of a system measuring their own
system is not the same evidence as an outside check.

**2. Who checks it.** Every system in `papers.md` has people between its output and any action. Say who
they are, and what happens when the system is wrong.

**3. A visual.** One picture the class can read in ten seconds. **Make it or find it, either is fine.**
A chart built from the numbers in your paper works well. If you make it, say what data it is built
from. If you find it, say who published it.
